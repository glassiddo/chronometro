FAQ

Which lines are included?

Paris

Included: Metro, RER A to E, and tram lines T1 to T14.

Excluded: buses, ORLYVAL, and CDG VAL.

London

Included: the 11 Underground lines and Elizabeth.

Excluded for now: London Overground, DLR, trams, buses, and national rail services. London is a preliminary version, so its fastest answer means the fastest route within the lines currently included.

Chicago

Included: Red, Blue, Brown, Green, Orange, Pink, Purple, and Yellow ‘L’ routes.

Excluded: CTA buses and Metra. Chicago uses scheduled static GTFS only, not live Train Tracker data.

Washington, DC

Included: Red, Blue, Orange, Silver, Yellow, and Green Metrorail lines.

Excluded: Metrobus, DC Circulator, MARC, VRE, Amtrak, commuter buses, and all other non-Metrorail services.

Why is the fastest route different from the one I know?

Chronométro follows a static route map and timing model. Ride times come from saved timetable data; waits and transfers are estimates. Direction and branches matter, so the return trip may have a different answer.

The game does not use live service patterns, disruptions, or real-time fast and semi-fast service decisions.

London: branch services currently share the line's estimated wait, rather than using a separate wait for every destination. Ordinary Underground changes use a fixed two-minute estimate; linked records within a larger station hub use three minutes. Real interchanges vary considerably, so these assumptions can change which route appears fastest.

How does it find the fastest route?

The game treats stations and connections as a weighted graph, then uses Dijkstra's algorithm to find the route with the lowest total time. This Veritasium video explains the basic idea.

When is walking an option?

The source transit data lists walking connections between some stations. The game offers a walk only when one of those links exists, or when separate records belong to the same station hub.

Paris: walking links and equivalent station records follow the saved GTFS data and the game's configured transfer rules.

London: linked platforms count as one station at the start and finish of a puzzle. This is deliberate: you can begin on the appropriate platform within the named station and finish on arrival at that station. Changing between those platforms midway through a route still uses the modeled interchange time.

Chicago: CTA platform/direction stop records are collapsed only through their GTFS parent_station. No proximity-based walking transfers are invented.

Washington: Farragut Crossing is the only modeled walk between distinct stations. The documented connection between Farragut North and Farragut West is modeled as a five-minute walk. No other stations are connected merely because they are nearby.

How are waiting times estimated?

Paris: the game looks at weekday departures from 7:00–10:00 AM, finds the median gap between services, and uses half of that as the expected wait. When there is not enough data for a direction, it falls back to the line and then the transport mode.

London: Underground waits use the same half-median-gap method with saved weekday morning timetable departures. Elizabeth uses a fixed expected wait of 2½ minutes because direction-level headways were not available in the saved source snapshot. When an entire ride can be made on either Circle or H&C, their frequencies are combined to estimate the wait for the first suitable train. The benefit is not applied after their routes diverge.

Chicago: waits use half the median gap between scheduled weekday departures from 7:00–10:00 AM. If a direction has too little data, the model falls back to the route and then a three-minute ‘L’ wait.

Washington: waits use the same half-median-gap method with scheduled weekday Metrorail departures from 7:00–10:00 AM. Fallbacks proceed from direction to line and then to a four-minute Metrorail wait.

The model adds a wait for the first service you board. It does not add time for entering the departure station or reaching your preferred starting platform.

Does the site save anything?

There are no accounts or cookies, and the game does not use local storage. Your route and scores disappear when you reload or close the page.
